# Testimonials

**Six verbatim testimonials.** Five are published on the client's own website; one is a public
review found on Word of Mouth.

**All are cleared for use in any channel, including paid advertising.** They are the client's
own published material on the client's own site. Use them verbatim, attributed exactly as
published.

> **The one residual check that isn't ours:** ad platforms apply their own rules to
> testimonials in financial-services advertising, and Meta in particular restricts creative
> that implies knowledge of a viewer's financial situation. That is a platform-policy check at
> ad-build time, not a permission question. See
> [paid-ads](../08-channels-and-playbooks/paid-ads.md).

## Samantha G and braydonn are the same argument, five years apart

> After the bank cancelled our unconditional approval, Karlie worked overnight… *(2026)*

> …when the banks wouldn't look twice. *(2019)*

Two independent customers, half a decade apart, describing the same thing: **a bank said no or
withdrew, and a person fought it.** That is not a service claim, it is a pattern — and it is
the one thing a comparison site and a bank branch structurally cannot replicate.

**Lead the "why not just go to the bank" and "I've already been declined" arguments with
these two.** See [competitor-bank-direct](../06-competitors-and-market/competitor-bank-direct.md).

## Usage rules

1. **Verbatim.** Do not edit for grammar, tighten, merge or modernise.
2. **Attribution as published** — first name plus last initial, or the handle as shown. Never
   enrich with a full name, suburb, photo or loan detail found elsewhere.
3. **Never construct** a testimonial, a composite, or a "representative" quote.
4. **Never imply volume.** Six testimonials is not "hundreds of happy clients". See
   [what-we-cannot-claim](what-we-cannot-claim.md).
5. **Never frame an outcome as typical or repeatable.** Samantha G's is a specific event, not
   a service guarantee.
6. Note Jaesen J's uses the singular *"The Loan Suite"*. Quote it as written — never correct a
   customer's words.
7. **Quote, don't adopt.** A customer may say "approved". We may not.

## Still uncaptured

The business almost certainly has more reviews than six. It has a Google Business Profile and
at least two Facebook pages, and reviews accumulate there by default. **Automated retrieval of
Google reviews is blocked** — Google serves a bot check to any automated request, so this has
to be exported by someone signed in to the profile.

A search snapshot on 27 Aug 2026 surfaced further review text mentioning *"Karlie, Jess, Lisa
and Carms"* on a refinance and *"Karlie and Jess"* on a first home loan. **Neither was read at
source, so neither is recorded here as usable.** They are evidence that more reviews exist,
and a reason to do the export.

The closest competitor publishes **215+ Google reviews**. That gap is the most visible
competitive weakness in the business, and it is closed by an export plus a review-request step
in Michelle's post-settlement workflow — not by a campaign.
