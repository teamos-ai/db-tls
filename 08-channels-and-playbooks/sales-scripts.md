## The rule that matters most on a call

**Everything in [guardrails](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/guardrails.md) applies to spoken
words.** A broker who says "you'll definitely get approved" on a phone call has made a
representation. Live conversation is where compliance most often slips, because there is no
draft to check.

The three phrases that must never be said:
- *"You'll be approved."* → *"Based on what you've told me, this looks like something lenders
  may consider — but only they can decide."*
- *"I can get you X%."* → *"I can't quote a rate until I know which lender fits. What I can do
  is compare what's available for your situation."*
- *"Don't worry about that."* → address it or say you'll find out.

## The 15-minute discovery call

**Purpose:** establish whether there is a deal, what kind, and book the real conversation.
It is **not** a fact find and not a pitch.

**0–1 min · Set the frame**
> Thanks for making time. This'll take about fifteen minutes. I just want to understand what
> you're trying to do and whether we can help — and if we can't, I'll tell you that too.
> There's no cost either way.

**1–5 min · What are you actually trying to do**
- What's prompted you to look at this now? *(the trigger event)*
- What are you hoping to do — buying, refinancing, investing, something for the business?
- Is there a timeframe, or a property, or a deadline in play?
- **Listen for the real job.** They rarely lead with it.

**5–10 min · The picture, lightly**
- Roughly, what does your income look like? Employed, self-employed, mix?
- Any existing loans or commitments?
- Have you spoken to anyone else about this — your bank, another broker?
- **Do not run numbers on the call.** Gather enough to know if it's viable.

**10–13 min · What happens next**
> Here's what I'd suggest. I'll take what you've told me, run it properly against our lender
> panel, and come back with what looks realistic. That means a slightly longer conversation
> and a few documents — payslips, bank statements, that sort of thing.
>
> Before that I'll send you our Credit Guide, which explains how we work and how we're paid.

**13–15 min · Book the next step and answer the money question**
> One thing people often want to ask and don't — this doesn't cost you anything. The lender
> pays us when a loan settles. If nothing settles, we don't get paid, and you're not out of
> pocket either way.

## Do

- Ask about the trigger event first. It tells you urgency and emotion
- Let silence sit after a question
- **Say "I don't know, I'll find out"** — it builds more trust than a confident guess
- Name the next step and the date before hanging up
- Address the cost question even if they don't ask. It's the unspoken one
- Take a file note. It's a BID obligation, not admin

## Don't

- Don't quote a rate, a borrowing capacity or a repayment on a discovery call
- Don't say approved, guaranteed, qualify, or "no problem"
- Don't run a serviceability calculation live and read out the number
- Don't badmouth their bank or their last broker
- Don't push someone in hardship toward a refinance — see
  [disqualifiers](../03-audience-and-icp/disqualifiers.md)
- Don't leave without a booked next step or an explicit "not now, come back in X"

## Pre-call checklist

- [ ] I know which ICP this is and their likely trigger
- [ ] I know the two or three objections most likely to come up
- [ ] I know which of the three CTAs is the right next step
- [ ] I will not quote a figure
- [ ] I know where the booking routes and who owns the file after this
- [ ] If hardship surfaces, I know the referral: **National Debt Helpline 1800 007 007**

## Worked example — the "I'll just ask my bank" objection

**Them:** *"I was going to just call my bank first and see what they can do."*

**Response:**
> You absolutely should — genuinely, ask them. Sometimes they'll move, and if they do that's
> a good outcome with no effort.
>
> Two things worth knowing before you do. Most lenders price new customers better than
> existing ones, so what they offer you to stay is often not their sharpest number. And
> whatever they come back with is one lender's view — it doesn't tell you what the other sixty
> would do.
>
> So here's what I'd suggest: ask them, and let me look at the same time. It costs you
> nothing, and then you're comparing their best offer against the market instead of against
> nothing.

---

**Why it works:** agrees rather than argues, gives them a genuinely useful fact, reframes to
"do both", removes cost as a barrier, no rate quoted, no disparagement of a named lender.
