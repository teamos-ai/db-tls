# Playbook — newsletter

> **No newsletter currently exists.** The cadence and topic pillars below come from
> operational planning and are a proposed starting point, not an established format.

## Purpose

A recurring broadcast to the full list — past clients, nurture pool, partners — that keeps The
Loans Suite present between transactions. **Its job is retention and referral, not acquisition.**

## Format

- **Cadence:** fortnightly. Weekly is unsustainable for a six-person team; monthly is forgettable
- **Length:** 400–700 words total
- **Sections:** three, always the same three, so it becomes recognisable
- **From:** Karlie, personally. The whole point is that it is from a person

## The three-section structure

**1. What's happening (150–250 words).** One thing that changed and what it means for the
reader. An RBA decision, a scheme change, a lending rule. **Every figure sourced and dated.**

**2. The useful bit (150–250 words).** One practical thing they can act on. This is the
section that earns the open next time.

**3. Something human (80–150 words).** A settlement the team was pleased about (de-identified,
no figures), a team update, something Karlie noticed. **This is the section that makes it a
newsletter rather than a circular** — and it is the one most likely to be cut under time
pressure. Don't cut it.

Close with one CTA — usually "reply if you want me to look at yours."

## Topic pillars

| Pillar | Example |
|---|---|
| **What the RBA decision actually means** | Translate the cash rate into a repayment on a real loan size |
| **Front-book vs back-book** | Why the rate your bank offers new customers isn't the one you have |
| **Rule and scheme changes** | Payday Super, SMSF restrictions, stamp duty changes by state |
| **Local market** | Moreton Bay and western Sydney — sourced figures only |
| **Behind the deal** | De-identified, structural, no numbers. *How* something got solved |
| **Business finance** | The under-served half of the list |

## Do

- Write it as one person to one person
- Source and date every figure
- Translate, don't report — the reader can get the news anywhere; they can't get "here's what
  it means for your repayment" anywhere
- Keep the same three sections every time
- Include business finance regularly — a third of the offer, almost none of the content

## Don't

- Don't publish a rate, or imply we have rates to offer
- Don't use an unsourced statistic
- Don't include a client story with figures, or any identifying detail
- Don't turn it into a listicle roundup of other people's articles
- Don't send it from "The Loans Suite Team"
- Don't reference clawback windows or retention timing

## Pre-send checklist

- [ ] Every figure appears in [statistics-and-sources](../05-proof-and-evidence/statistics-and-sources.md), with source and period
- [ ] No figure is presented as a Loans Suite result
- [ ] Zero banned words; no rate or approval language
- [ ] Any client reference is de-identified and figure-free
- [ ] Three sections present, including the human one
- [ ] One CTA
- [ ] From Karlie, with her real sign-off
- [ ] Unsubscribe + both compliance lines
- [ ] 400–700 words

## Worked example — section 1

**Subject:** What the 81% figure actually tells you

Four out of five Australians who took out a home loan last quarter used a broker.

That's the MFAA's number for the March 2026 quarter — 81%, a record, up from 76.8% a year
earlier. Eight years ago it was 55%.

I don't think that's really a story about brokers. I think it's a story about how much harder
lending has got. Every lender assesses income differently now. The buffer means your borrowing
capacity is smaller than the rate suggests. And the rate your bank gives a new customer is
usually not the one you're on.

None of that is obvious from the outside. It's just a lot of moving parts, and most people
would rather not learn them.

Worth knowing: the same report puts Australia alongside the UK and the Netherlands as the only
three countries where brokers do more than 80% of mortgage lending.

*Source: MFAA Quarterly Market Share Report, data by Cotality, March 2026 quarter.*
