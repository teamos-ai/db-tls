Why home loan repayments matter

A home loan is usually a long-term financial commitment, so it is important to understand whether the repayments fit comfortably within your budget. Even a small difference in interest rate, loan amount or loan term can change your repayment amount over time.

Before applying for a home loan, it is worth considering:

How much you may be able to borrow

What your estimated repayments could be

Whether weekly, fortnightly or monthly repayments suit your cash flow

Whether a fixed, variable or split loan may suit your needs

How changes in interest rates could affect your budget

Whether refinancing could reduce your repayments or improve your loan structure

A calculator gives you a useful estimate, but it does not assess your full situation or replace personalised lending advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is a home loan repayment calculator?

A home loan repayment calculator estimates how much your mortgage repayments could be based on details such as the loan amount, interest rate, loan term and repayment frequency.

Are home loan repayment calculator results accurate?

A calculator provides an estimate only. Your actual repayments may vary depending on the lender, interest rate, fees, loan type, repayment structure and final loan approval terms.

Should I choose weekly, fortnightly or monthly repayments?

The right repayment frequency depends on your income cycle and budgeting preferences. Some borrowers prefer weekly or fortnightly repayments to align with their pay, while others prefer monthly repayments for simplicity.

What affects my home loan repayments?

Your repayments may be affected by the loan amount, interest rate, loan term, repayment type, fees and whether your loan is fixed, variable or split.

Can refinancing reduce my repayments?

Refinancing may help reduce repayments if you can access a lower interest rate, extend your loan term or restructure your loan. However, refinancing can also involve costs, so it is important to compare the overall benefit.
