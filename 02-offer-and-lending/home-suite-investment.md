# Investment property loans

## The promise, verbatim

> Investing in property can be a powerful way to build long-term wealth, but the right loan
> structure matters.

> Investment lending is not just about the interest rate. The Loans Suite can help you
> consider repayment types, equity, offset options and loan structures that align with your
> broader property plans.

This is where the "financial architects" positioning is strongest — structure genuinely
does more work than rate for a portfolio builder.

## What the site says to know before investing

- How ongoing costs, rental income and repayments may impact cash flow
- How lender policies can differ for investment properties
- What documents and details you may need before applying

## Published FAQs — canonical answers

**Can I use equity to buy an investment property?**
> Yes, many property investors use existing equity as part of their deposit or purchase
> strategy. The amount available will depend on your property value, current loan balance,
> income, expenses and lender requirements.

**Do lenders include rental income when assessing my loan?**
> In many cases, yes. Lenders may include expected or existing rental income, but they often
> assess it at a reduced percentage to allow for vacancies, costs and risk.

**Should I choose principal and interest or interest-only repayments?**
> Both options can suit different strategies. Principal and interest repayments help reduce
> the loan balance over time, while interest-only repayments may help with short-term cash
> flow, depending on your goals and circumstances.

Paraphrase freely; **never change the substance or the hedging**.

## The entry tool

The page's primary CTA is **"Check your equity"** → the
[equity cashout calculator](calculators-and-tools.md). That is the natural top of funnel for
this audience: an existing owner discovering they can move before they thought they could.

## Copy rules

- Never promise capital growth, rental yield or a tax outcome.
- Negative gearing and depreciation are **tax matters** — refer to an accountant, do not explain.
- The brand's own wealth language is safe because it is aspirational rather than predictive:
  *"dream bigger, pay down your home loan sooner, and build long-term wealth through smart
  property investing."*
