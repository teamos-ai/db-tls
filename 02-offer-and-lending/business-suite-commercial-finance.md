# Commercial finance

## The promise, verbatim

> Commercial finance can help businesses purchase property, fund growth, refinance existing
> debt or unlock capital for the next stage.

> Commercial finance can involve different entities, securities, income sources and lender
> policies.

## What it covers

Purchasing commercial premises · refinancing business or property debt · funding expansion ·
supporting cash flow · buying equipment · fit-outs.

The page is explicit that this is broader than property:

> No. Commercial finance can include property lending, business lending, asset finance,
> working capital facilities and other funding options depending on the business need.

## What lenders consider

> Lenders may consider business income, leases, security, trading history and financial
> statements.

> Some commercial finance options may require a **larger deposit or lower loan-to-value ratio**.

Documents that may be requested: financial statements, tax returns, BAS, bank statements,
lease agreements, business plans, property details, information about existing debts.

## Why this line carries the positioning

Commercial is **first** in the homepage's solutions ordering, and it is Karlie's own
specialisation — her published title is **Commercial & Residential Broker**, and her bio
names *"Residential and Commercial Lending, Car & Equipment Finance, and Self-Managed Super
Fund Lending"*.

The credibility argument: most residential brokers cannot do commercial. A brokerage that
handles both can take a business owner's whole picture — their home, their premises, their
equipment — rather than handing half of it to someone else. **This is the "financial
architects" claim at its most defensible.**

## Copy rules

- Complexity is the selling point, but never imply we can solve a deal a bank declined.
  "We can help you understand what lenders may need" — the site's own hedge — is the ceiling.
- Never state an LVR, a deposit percentage or a rate.
- Commercial borrowers are generally business-purpose, which sits outside the consumer
  credit regime — but **write to the consumer standard anyway**. See
  [nccp-and-best-interests-duty](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/nccp-and-best-interests-duty.md).
