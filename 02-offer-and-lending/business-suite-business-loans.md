# Business loans

## The promise, verbatim

> Every business needs funding at different stages, whether it is for cash flow, growth,
> stock, equipment, expansion or a new opportunity.

## Uses named on the page

Working capital · expansion · stock purchases · fit-outs · equipment · vehicles ·
refinancing existing business debt · marketing

## What lenders assess

> Each lender assesses business lending differently… what information may be needed,
> including **financials, BAS, tax returns, bank statements or cash flow details**.

> Lenders may assess cash flow, trading history and existing debts.

Some loans are secured, some unsecured — the page is explicit that it varies.

## Published FAQs — canonical answers

**Do I need security for a business loan?**
> Not always. Some lenders offer unsecured business loans, while others may require
> residential property, commercial property, business assets or other forms of security.

**How quickly can business finance be approved?**
> Approval timeframes vary depending on the lender, loan type, application strength and
> documents required. **Some business loans can move in as little as a few hours**, while
> more complex applications may take longer.

That "few hours" figure is the site's own and is properly hedged. Reproduce the hedge with
it, always. Never write "approved in hours" as a promise.

## The strategic opening

The business's own blog has already identified the argument, in
*Five SME lending trends every Australian business should know in 2026*:

> SMEs are increasingly looking for a broker who can help them navigate **all** their
> capital needs, whether that's traditional bank loans, non-bank solutions, asset finance,
> lines of credit or blended funding options.

The Loans Suite can credibly claim to be that adviser — it holds home, commercial, business
and asset finance under one roof, and the same principal handles a business owner's home
loan and their equipment. **The "full-stack finance adviser" framing is the strongest
untapped positioning in the business.**

## Timely hooks with real dates

From the same blog post, both `verified` against the site's own publication:

- **Payday Super — in force since 1 July 2026.** Employers must pay super at the same time as
  wages, and the contribution must **reach the fund within 7 BUSINESS days** of payday,
  replacing the quarterly cycle. Calculated on **"qualifying earnings"** at **12%**, every pay run.
- **ATO compliance pressure** driving businesses to seek finance to meet obligations.

> **Correction, verified 2026-08-27.** The client's blog says *"within seven calendar days of
> payday"* and frames the change as forthcoming. It is **seven business days**, and it has been
> in force for nearly two months. Both need fixing — see
> [regulatory-changes-2026](../06-competitors-and-market/regulatory-changes-2026.md).

Payday Super remains the single best business-finance campaign trigger available — and it is
better now than when the blog was written, because it is **already biting** rather than pending.
It converts a quarterly cash outflow into a per-payday one, which is a working-capital event.

**Never give payroll, tax or super advice.** Describe the change; refer them to their accountant.
