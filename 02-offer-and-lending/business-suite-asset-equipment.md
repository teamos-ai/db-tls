# Asset & equipment finance

## The promise, verbatim

> The right equipment can help your business grow, improve efficiency or take on new
> opportunities, but paying upfront is not always the best option.

> Asset finance can help spread the cost of equipment over time instead of tying up working
> capital.

## What it covers

Vehicles · machinery · tools · technology · specialist equipment.

## What affects the outcome

> The **age, value and type** of asset can affect your finance options.

> Loan terms, **balloon payments** and ownership structures can change the overall cost.

## Published FAQs — canonical answers

**Do I need to provide full financials?**
> Not always. Some lenders may offer **low-doc or alternative verification** options,
> depending on the business, asset, loan amount and overall application strength.

**Can I finance used equipment?**
> Yes, used equipment may be eligible for finance, but lenders will usually consider the
> asset's age, condition, value and expected useful life.

The used-equipment answer matters — it is a real differentiator against dealer finance,
which typically only funds new.

## Strategic role

Asset finance is **second** in the homepage ordering, ahead of home loans. It is:

- **The fastest cycle in the business.** A vehicle or equipment deal closes in days, not months.
- **The lowest-friction entry to a business owner.** Nobody agonises over a ute the way they
  agonise over a mortgage — which makes it the cheapest first transaction in a relationship
  that later includes commercial premises and a home loan.
- **Cross-sellable from every other line.** A construction client needs machinery. A business
  loan client needs vehicles. A home loan client needs a car.

Karlie's own bio names **"Car & Equipment Finance"** as a specialisation, which supports
leading with it.

The blog already covers this audience — three asset-finance posts exist, including
*"Should you pay cash or finance your new car"* and *"Using asset finance for your medical or
dental practice"*. The latter points at a real vertical: **professional practices**.

## Copy rules

- Never state a rate, a balloon percentage or a term.
- Balloon payments must be described neutrally — they lower repayments and raise total cost.
  Never present one as a saving.
- Tax treatment (instant asset write-off, depreciation) is an **accountant's** matter.
  Mention that it exists; never explain or quantify it.
