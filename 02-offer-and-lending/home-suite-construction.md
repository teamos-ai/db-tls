# Construction loans

## The promise, verbatim

> Building a home or completing a major renovation usually needs a different type of finance
> to a standard property purchase.

> Construction lending has extra moving parts, including contracts, plans, valuations and
> progress payment stages.

## Covers

New builds, renovations, and knockdown rebuilds.

## What the site says lenders need

- A **fixed-price building contract**, plans and council approvals
- Funds released **in stages**, not as one lump sum
- Changes to build, budget or timeline may affect approval or drawdowns

## Published FAQs — canonical answers

**How does a construction loan work?**
> A construction loan usually releases funds in stages as each part of the build is
> completed. These stages may include **slab, frame, lock-up, fixing and completion**,
> depending on the lender and builder contract.

**Do I need a fixed-price building contract?**
> Most lenders prefer or require a fixed-price building contract because it helps confirm
> the cost of the project. Some lenders may consider other arrangements, but requirements
> can vary.

**Do I pay interest on the full loan amount straight away?**
> Usually, interest is charged only on the amount drawn down during construction. This means
> repayments may increase as more funds are released throughout the build.

That last answer resolves the single most common misunderstanding in construction lending
and is a strong standalone content hook.

## Copy rules

- Never state a drawdown timeline or a valuation outcome.
- Never guarantee a builder's progress claim will be released.
- Stage names (slab, frame, lock-up, fixing, completion) are safe — they are the site's own
  and are industry-standard.
