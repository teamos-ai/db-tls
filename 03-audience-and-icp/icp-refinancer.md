# ICP — the refinancer

> **Tier note:** `inferred`. Reasoned from the business's own tools and content, not from
> prospect interviews.

**Commercially the most important audience in the business.** Fastest to settle, least
educational work, an existing property as security, and — via the retention pipeline — a
pool the business already owns.

## Who they are

30–55. Owns a home, has had the same loan for three or more years, has never asked their
bank for a better rate. Two distinct sub-segments that need opposite messages:

**The squeezed refinancer.** Repayments have become uncomfortable. Came off a fixed rate,
or rates moved. The mortgage stress calculator is built for them. Anxious, slightly ashamed,
avoiding the topic.

**The strategic refinancer.** Comfortable, but the loan has drifted. Wants to release equity
for a renovation, an investment property, or to consolidate. The equity cashout calculator is
built for them. Curious, opportunistic, moves fast when shown a number.

## Trigger events

- Coming off a fixed rate — **the single sharpest trigger in the category**
- An RBA decision in the news
- A rate rise landing in their repayment
- A property revaluation or a neighbour's sale price
- Wanting to renovate or buy an investment
- A bank's loyalty rate quietly diverging from its new-customer rate

## The job to be done

**"Tell me whether I'm being taken advantage of, and make fixing it not feel like a project."**

Refinancing is universally understood to be worth doing and universally postponed, because
the perceived cost is *effort*, not money. Whoever removes the effort wins.

## The fears that block the decision

1. **"It'll be a hassle."** The number one reason people don't refinance. The site's answer —
   *"we handle the paperwork, liaise with the lender"* — is the whole pitch.
2. **"My bank will match it anyway, so why bother."**
3. **"There'll be exit fees or costs that eat the saving."** Legitimate; the site is honest
   about it: *"it is important to consider fees, costs and whether refinancing is suitable."*
4. **"I'll be rejected and it'll hurt my credit."**
5. **"I don't know if my rate is actually bad."** Most people genuinely do not know.

## The business's own best hook for this audience

> **If your rate hasn't changed in years, you could be paying too much.**

And the named offer nobody is promoting:

> **Loan Health Check** — Available for when you already have a loan. Let us review it and
> show you if there's a better deal.

The blog also carries a strong dated angle: *"The Covid Rate Trap – Are You Caught In It?"* —
*"If you bought your home between 2020 and 2022, there's a very good chance your loan was
assessed under conditions that no longer exist."* That is a precise, time-bounded, highly
targetable cohort.

## What converts them

- **A specific number about their own loan**, produced in under two minutes.
- **"We do the paperwork."** Repeat it. It is the actual objection.
- **A deadline or an event** — fixed rate expiry, an RBA date.
- **Not asking them to switch yet.** "Let's see if it's worth it" beats "refinance now".

## Disqualifiers

Recently refinanced (under ~12 months), likely to be selling within 12 months, or in genuine
hardship — that is a hardship conversation with their lender and the National Debt Helpline
(**1800 007 007**, which the site itself publishes), not a refinance pitch.
See [disqualifiers](disqualifiers.md).
