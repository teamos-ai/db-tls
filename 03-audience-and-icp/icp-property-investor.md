# ICP — the property investor

> **Tier note:** `inferred`. Reasoned from published copy and tooling.

## Who they are

35–60. Owns a home with meaningful equity. Either about to buy their first investment
property or building a portfolio of two to four. Financially literate but not expert —
they read, they listen to podcasts, they have opinions about structure that may or may not
be right.

The brand speaks to them directly on the homepage:

> We're here to help you dream bigger, pay down your home loan sooner, and build long-term
> wealth through smart property investing.

## Trigger events

- A revaluation or a neighbour's sale revealing equity they didn't know they had
- Approaching a milestone birthday and thinking about retirement income
- Hitting a **serviceability wall** with their current lender and being told no
- A tax bill that prompts a conversation with their accountant
- A property they've seen and want to move on quickly

## The job to be done

**"Structure this so I can keep going, not just get this one over the line."**

This is the audience for whom "financial architects" is not marketing language — it is
literally the requirement. The wrong structure on property two closes off properties three
and four.

## The fears that block the decision

1. **"I'll hit a serviceability wall."** The real constraint for anyone past property two.
2. **"I'll cross-collateralise and regret it."** Widely feared, poorly understood.
3. **"Interest-only versus P&I — I'll pick wrong."** The site answers this neutrally.
4. **"Rates will move and the numbers stop working."**
5. **"My accountant and my broker will give me different answers."**

## Where The Loans Suite is genuinely strong here

**Panel breadth is a real answer to a serviceability wall.** When one lender says no, having
60+ lenders with materially different servicing calculators is not a marketing claim — it is
the difference between buying and not buying. This is the one audience for whom the panel
number is the argument rather than a commodity claim.

**Commercial capability compounds it.** An investor who eventually moves into commercial
property does not need a new broker.

## What converts them

- **A number about their own equity.** The equity cashout calculator, with its intent
  segmentation, is purpose-built for this.
- **A structural insight** they hadn't considered — offset placement, splitting, ordering
  of purchases.
- **Speed.** They move on opportunities and need a broker who answers the phone.
- **Working with their accountant** rather than around them.

## Disqualifiers

Anyone seeking property investment *advice* — which property, which suburb, whether to buy.
The Loans Suite arranges finance; it does not advise on investments. Anyone whose plan
depends on a tax outcome goes to their accountant first.
See [guardrails](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/guardrails.md).

## Copy rules

- Never project capital growth, rental yield or a return.
- Never explain negative gearing or depreciation — name that they exist, refer to an accountant.
- The site's canonical FAQ answers on equity, rental income and P&I versus interest-only are
  in [home-suite-investment](../02-offer-and-lending/home-suite-investment.md). Paraphrase,
  never re-reason.
