# Buyer psychology

## The one thing to understand about this category

**Nobody wants a loan.** They want the house, the equipment, the investment, or to stop
worrying. The loan is the obstacle between them and the thing. Every piece of copy that
treats the loan as the product is talking about the wrong subject.

The brand already knows this — *"Helping you achieve your next big goal in life, property or
business!"* sits in the footer of every page.

## The three fears, named by the business itself

The blog post *"Are FOMO FOBO and FOOP Holding You Back?"* is the closest thing the business
has to a psychological framework, and it is genuinely useful:

**FOMO — Fear of Missing Out**
> the feeling that if you don't act now, you'll lose your chance… FOMO can push buyers into
> rushing a purchase before they're really ready.

**FOBO — Fear of Better Options**
> when you second-guess every choice, worrying that a better property or loan might be around
> the corner. FOBO can stall decision-making.

**FOOP — Fear of Over-Paying**
> when buyers worry they'll pay too much and regret it later… it can also keep you sitting on
> the sidelines.

The post's own resolution is the right one and is compliance-safe:

> The key is awareness. Recognising FOMO, FOBO or FOOP in yourself gives you the chance to
> pause and reset.

**Note what it does not do: it never uses the fear to push a decision.** That restraint is
both ethically correct and — for a regulated product — mandatory. See
[guardrails](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/guardrails.md) on urgency framing.

## Shame is the hidden blocker

Across every segment, the emotion that stops the first contact is not fear of a bad rate.
It is **embarrassment**:

- The first home buyer who doesn't know what LMI stands for
- The refinancer who has been on the same rate for six years and knows they should have moved
- The business owner whose books are a mess
- The investor who got the structure wrong on property two

**Copy that reduces embarrassment outperforms copy that increases urgency.** The first-home
page already does it: *"You won't be left guessing or trying to piece it together on your
own… just real answers."*

## What the testimonials reveal about what people actually value

The five published testimonials mention, in order of frequency:

1. **Speed and simplicity** — *"super-fast and simple"*
2. **Communication and explanation** — *"Really good communication and easily explained all
   the processes"*, *"answered in a fast and easy to comprehend way"*
3. **Repeat use** — *"a few times now"*, *"the second time we've used"*
4. **Rescue** — *"After the bank cancelled our unconditional approval, Karlie worked overnight
   and over a few days to save us from losing our new house and deposit"*

**Not one mentions a rate.** Four out of five are about the experience. The fifth is about
someone fighting for them when things went wrong.

That is the actual value proposition, in customers' own words, sitting unused on the homepage.

## The three-tier commitment ladder

The site's own CTA structure maps to psychological readiness and should be respected rather
than collapsed:

**Explore** (anonymous, no commitment) → **Talk** (a person, still no commitment) →
**Apply** (documents, real commitment)

Pushing someone from Explore to Apply in one step is where funnels break. The calculators
exist to serve the first rung; only two of the seven capture anything at all.

## What the principal says about her own clients — verified, 9 Sep 2026

The first primary-source statement about how different client types want to be treated:

> I don't want clients notified every single time the tile moves along just the milestones.

> First home buyers. Yes, that's really good for them because they like to be in the loop. But a lot
> of my clients are not first time buyers… I could have commercial clients going through here as
> well. And the last thing they want is to be bombarded with reminders and SMSs and emails.
> — Karlie Scharfenberg

**Implication:** communication appetite differs sharply by segment. First home buyers want
reassurance and frequent updates; commercial, investor and repeat clients read frequent automated
contact as noise. Any sequence must set frequency by segment. See
[automation-requirements](../11-operations/automation-requirements.md).
