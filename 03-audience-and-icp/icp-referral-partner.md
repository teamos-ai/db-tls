# ICP — the referral partner

> **Tier note:** `inferred`. No partner has been interviewed and no referral arrangement is
> documented. This profile is reasoned from who the twelve published partners are.

## Who they are

Four professional categories, each seeing a client at a different point in the buying cycle:

| Partner type | Sees the client | What they need from us |
|---|---|---|
| **Real estate agents** | Earliest — at inspection, before finance | A buyer who can actually transact, fast, so the deal doesn't fall over on finance |
| **Conveyancers / solicitors** | At contract | Finance approved before the finance clause expires; a broker who answers the phone |
| **Accountants / financial planners** | Continuously | A broker who won't blow up a structure they built, and who refers back |
| **Adjacent specialists** (credit repair, deposit bonds, insurance) | When a client is blocked | A pipeline of clients who need unblocking |

## The job to be done

**"Give me a broker I can hand a client to without it becoming my problem."**

A referral is a reputational loan. The partner is lending their client relationship. What
they fear is not a bad rate — it is silence, a missed deadline, or a client who comes back
annoyed at *them*.

## What they actually want, in order

1. **Communication.** Being told where the file is without having to chase.
2. **Speed and reliability.** Finance clauses have dates.
3. **The client treated well.** It reflects on them.
4. **Reciprocity.** Referrals coming back the other way.
5. **Commercials.** Genuinely last, and often not present at all — most professional
   referrals are relationship-based, and for some categories fee-sharing is restricted.

The Loans Suite already does #4 structurally: the partner directory sends clients *to*
partners. That is real reciprocity, published, and almost nobody in the category does it.
**It is the strongest partner-acquisition asset the business owns and it is invisible.**

## The disclosure boundary

Milestone updates to a partner are the mechanism that satisfies #1 — and the compliance
risk that comes with it. A partner may be told the status of *their referral*, never the
contents of the client's file.
**Read [privacy-and-data](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/privacy-and-data.md) before
designing any partner notification.**

## Trigger events for winning a new partner

- A deal of theirs just fell over on finance
- Their current broker went quiet on a file
- They are new to an area or a practice and building a network
- We referred them a client first — the strongest opener available

## What converts them

- **Referring them a client before asking for anything.** Reciprocity-first is both the most
  effective and the most defensible opening.
- **A specific promise about communication**, not a generic partnership pitch.
- **Proof of competence a professional recognises** — the seven award badges, and commercial
  capability their residential-only alternatives lack.
- **Being easy.** One phone number, one page, one process.

## What kills it

- A generic "let's do a referral partnership" cold email. Every professional gets several a week.
- Anything that sounds like it creates work for them.
- Any implied fee arrangement where none is documented — see
  [referral-partner-network](../02-offer-and-lending/referral-partner-network.md).

## Copy rules

- Never imply an existing partner referred anyone or endorses us beyond what their page says.
- Never state or imply a referral fee.
- Address them as professional peers, not as a lead source.
