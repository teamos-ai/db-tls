# Disqualifiers

## Hard — cannot proceed

| Situation | Why | Where they go |
|---|---|---|
| **Under 18**, or not an Australian citizen, PR or NZ citizen | Published personal loan eligibility | — |
| **Borrowing under $2,000** | Published minimum | — |
| **New residential SMSF borrowing** | Restricted from **10 August 2026** | Their accountant / SMSF specialist |
| **Wants financial, tax or superannuation advice** | Outside the credit representative's authorisation | Accountant, financial adviser, SMSF specialist |
| **Wants investment advice** — which property, which suburb | Not a licensed activity for this business | Licensed adviser or buyer's agent |
| **Credit repair** | Not a service The Loans Suite provides | Clear Credit Solutions (partner) |

## Hardship — a referral, never a pitch

Anyone in genuine financial hardship — behind on repayments, facing default, unable to meet
obligations — is **not a refinance lead**. Refinancing a distressed borrower into a new loan
can worsen their position and is exactly what the regime exists to prevent.

The site already handles this correctly and its own words should be the template:

> If you find yourself facing mortgage stress, it's crucial to seek guidance from a trusted
> expert… In addition, consider leveraging the free financial counselling services available
> in your state, such as the **National Debt Helpline on 1800 007 007**.

**Any campaign touching mortgage stress must carry that referral.** A mortgage-stress
calculator that returns a high-risk result and offers only a sales call is an ethical and
regulatory problem. It must also offer the helpline.

## Soft — proceed with care, or decline well

- **Defaults, judgments or bankruptcy on file.** Published criteria says *"ideally"* clean —
  a hedge, not a bar. The right move is often the Clear Credit referral first, then finance later.
- **Pre-revenue business with no trading history and no security.**
- **Recently refinanced (under ~12 months).** Costs likely outweigh benefit; revisit later.
- **Selling within 12 months.** Refinancing rarely makes sense.
- **Shopping purely on rate**, unwilling to discuss structure. Not a bad person — a bad fit
  for a structuring-led brokerage, and likely to churn.
- **Wants a decision today with no documents.** Expectation mismatch.

## How to decline well

The category default is to go quiet. That is the worst option — it produces the exact
experience that drives people to complain about brokers.

1. **Say no clearly and quickly.** Ambiguity costs them time.
2. **Say why, in one sentence**, without diagnosing them.
3. **Give them the next step** — a partner, a helpline, a timeframe.
4. **Leave the door open with a date.** *"Come back to me in six months and we'll look again"*
   is a real re-engagement trigger, not a brush-off.
5. **Never blame the lender or the client.**

A well-handled decline is a referral source. A person told honestly that they are not ready
tells other people who *are* ready.
