# Brand voice

## The voice in one paragraph

Warm, plain-spoken and Australian. It talks *to* a person, not at a market. Short sentences,
second person, contractions. It is confident without being slick — closer to a capable friend
who happens to know lending than to a financial institution. It admits when something is
complicated and when it doesn't have the full answer. Its one piece of elevated language is
**"financial architects"**, and that phrase carries the whole premium positioning so nothing
else has to.

## The test for any sentence

> **Would Karlie say this out loud to a client sitting across the table, without sounding
> like a brochure?**

If it needs a corporate register to work, rewrite it.

## The five voice rules

**1. Second person, always.** "You", "your", "we". Never "clients are able to" or
"borrowers should consider". The site is almost perfectly consistent on this.

**2. Short sentences. Then a shorter one.**
> There's a lot to figure out when you're starting out. Grants, deposits, borrowing power,
> loan types. It can feel like a lot. That's where we come in.

**3. Admit complexity rather than smoothing it over.**
> Limited information available on this one!

> SMSF lending has become more complex, particularly for residential property.

This is the most distinctive thing the brand does. Most finance copy pretends everything is
simple. Honesty about difficulty reads as competence.

**4. Hedge where the truth requires it, and don't apologise for hedging.** "May", "could",
"depending on your circumstances". This is compliance and honesty doing the same job. Never
write around a hedge to make a sentence sound stronger.

**5. Australian English, always.** Recognise, organise, personalised, favourable, licence
(noun) / license (verb), maximise. Note the site itself slips into American spelling in
places — *"favorable"*, *"specializes"*, *"utilizing"* — in Karlie's and other bios.
**Correct this in anything new.**

## Register by audience

| Audience | Register |
|---|---|
| First home buyer | Warmest. Reassuring. Explain everything. *"Small steps today. Big wins tomorrow."* |
| Refinancer | Direct and practical. Lead with the number. Remove the hassle. |
| Investor | Peer-to-peer. Assume competence. Talk structure. |
| Business owner | Shortest and bluntest. Get to the point. Their time is the cost. |
| Referral partner | Professional peer. No sales language. |
| Sub-broker recruit | Candid, slightly informal, a bit of swagger. *"the runs on the board."* |

## Rhythm and structure

- **Lead with the reader's situation, not the business's capability.** The site's best pages
  open on the reader — *"Buying your first home is exciting, but…"* — and its weakest open on
  the business.
- **One idea per paragraph.** Two to four sentences.
- **Use the brand's own list rhythm:** a short lead-in, then a scannable list, then a human
  line to close.
- **Close on an invitation, not an instruction.** *"Let's chat"* beats *"Apply now"*. The
  site's CTAs are consistently conversational: *"Let's talk commercial"*, *"Help me build my
  portfolio"*, *"Let's build something together"*.

## The brand's own idioms — keep these

- **"Your financial architects"** — the central metaphor
- **"We do the legwork"**
- **"Zero-cost, zero-obligation"**
- **"Let's chat"** / **"Let's talk"**
- **"the runs on the board"**
- **"Hate maths? Lets us help you with that."** — plain, funny, on-brand (the typo is theirs)
- **"Small steps today. Big wins tomorrow."**

## Humour

Present, dry, and light. *"unless you really love reading, it just makes sense to pick up the
phone"*. *"The best things in life are free…like a consult with a loans suite broker. So
instead of DIY-ing life, let's chat."*

Use it in low-stakes contexts — calculators, social, general education. **Never** in SMSF,
hardship, mortgage stress or compliance copy.

## What the voice is not

- Not corporate. No "solutions-oriented", no "leverage", no "at the end of the day".
- Not hype. No exclamation stacking, no all-caps, no "GAME CHANGER".
- Not falsely premium. Despite the dark-and-gold visual identity, the copy is
  down-to-earth. **A mismatch here would be the single most likely way to get this brand
  wrong** — the design is boutique, the language is not.
- Not fear-based. See [buyer-psychology](../03-audience-and-icp/buyer-psychology.md).
