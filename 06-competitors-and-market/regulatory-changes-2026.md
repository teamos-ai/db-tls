# Regulatory changes in force — 2026

**Four changes landed in the twelve months to August 2026, and the client's website is out of
date on three of them.** Each is a campaign trigger and a correction at the same time.

Every item here has been checked at source. **Re-verify before each publication** — this is the
fastest-decaying file in the database.

---

## 1 · SMSF residential borrowing ban — NOW IN FORCE

**Source:** Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Bill 2026, Royal Assent 26 June 2026;
MFAA and multiple advisory summaries, researched 2026-08-27.

| | |
|---|---|
| Commenced | **10 August 2026** (45 days after Royal Assent) |
| What is banned | **New** limited recourse borrowing arrangements (LRBAs) to acquire **residential** property |
| Grandfathered | **Existing** SMSF residential LRBAs are unaffected |
| **Refinancing** | **Still permitted** on existing residential LRBAs, on substantially the same terms |
| Commercial | **Still permitted** — LRBAs for **business real property** remain available |
| Origin | Part of the Government–Greens agreement to pass the broader tax package |

### Why this matters commercially

**The site says the restriction "is being restricted from 10 August 2026" — future tense. It is
now in force.** Any SMSF asset written today must say so.

But the more useful finding is what **survived**:

1. **Refinancing existing SMSF residential loans is still allowed.** There is now a closed,
   finite population of SMSF trustees holding residential LRBAs who can never buy another one —
   and whose only remaining lever is refinancing. Their existing lenders know they cannot easily
   leave the product, which is exactly the condition under which back-book pricing drifts.
2. **Commercial SMSF lending is untouched.** A business owner buying their own premises through
   their SMSF is still a live transaction — and it needs an accountant in the room, which makes
   it the strongest B2B referral line in the business.

**The offer has pivoted from "SMSF property purchase" to "SMSF refinancing and commercial."**
The website has not.

### Copy rules

Unchanged and absolute — see [home-suite-smsf](../02-offer-and-lending/home-suite-smsf.md).
Never encourage a residential SMSF purchase. Never explain superannuation law. Always defer to
the accountant, financial adviser or SMSF specialist.

## 2 · Australian Government 5% Deposit Scheme — massively expanded

**Source:** Housing Australia, "Unlimited places, higher property price caps for first home
buyers from 1 October 2025"; researched 2026-08-27.

Renamed from the Home Guarantee Scheme on **1 October 2025**. Three changes, all large:

| | Before | From 1 Oct 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Places | Capped annually | **Uncapped** |
| Income caps | $125k single / $200k couple | **Removed entirely** |
| Deposit | 5% | 5%, **LMI avoided** |

### Property price caps — the two markets that matter

| | Capital city & regional centres | Other areas |
|---|---|---|
| **Queensland** | **$1,000,000** *(was $700,000)* | $700,000 *(was $550,000)* |
| **New South Wales** | **$1,500,000** *(was $900,000)* | $800,000 *(was $750,000)* |

### The angle nobody local is using

**Redcliffe's median house price is now approximately $1.0M** — see
[market-context](market-context.md). The Queensland cap is **$1,000,000**.

The scheme and the local median have converged. A first home buyer in Redcliffe, Rothwell or
Kippa-Ring is now buying **right at the cap**, which makes the difference between a $980,000
and a $1,020,000 property a difference between using the scheme and not. That is a genuinely
non-obvious, locally specific, useful piece of guidance — and it is exactly what a local broker
should be publishing and no national competitor will.

**Penrith is the opposite case:** a median around $880k–$1.1M against a $1.5M NSW cap means the
scheme is comfortably available across almost the whole market.

**The site's current copy on this scheme is generic and pre-dates all three changes.**

## 3 · Help to Buy — open, and only one lender is broker-accessible

**Source:** Housing Australia; Treasury; industry reporting, researched 2026-08-27.

| | |
|---|---|
| Launched | **5 December 2025.** All states and territories participating as at June 2026 |
| Places | **10,000** for 2026–27 |
| Income caps | Raised **1 July 2026** to **$110,000** individual, **$180,000** joint or single parent |
| Government equity | Up to **40%** of a new home, **30%** of an existing home |
| Participating lenders | **Commonwealth Bank** (branch only — **no broker access**) and **Bank Australia** (broker accessible) |

### The finding that matters most

**As at July 2026 there is exactly one broker-accessible lender on Help to Buy: Bank Australia.**

For a broker that is both a constraint and an opening. The constraint is obvious. The opening is
that most consumers have no idea the scheme exists, fewer know it is now open, and almost none
know that going to CBA's branch is the only other route. **Being the local broker who can
actually explain the scheme is a differentiated position** — precisely because so few brokers
can transact it.

**The site's current copy says: *"Limited information available on this one!"*** That was honest
when written. It is now simply out of date, and it is the single most fixable content gap on the
website.

## 4 · Payday Super — in force, and the client's own blog has it wrong

**Source:** ATO, "About Payday Super" and "Payment deadlines for Payday Super"; Pitcher Partners;
researched 2026-08-27.

| | |
|---|---|
| Commenced | **1 July 2026** — in force now, not upcoming |
| Deadline | Contribution must **reach the fund within 7 BUSINESS days** of payday |
| Basis | Calculated on **"qualifying earnings"** — a new term |
| Rate | **12%** super guarantee, applied every pay run |

**Qualifying earnings** brings together ordinary time earnings plus directors' fees, payments to
contractors wholly or principally for labour, and certain payments to artists, musicians and
sportspeople. **Overtime, termination lump sums and reimbursements are excluded.**

### Correction required

The client's blog post *Five SME lending trends every Australian business should know in 2026*
states the payment must reach the fund **"within seven calendar days of payday."**

**It is seven *business* days.** The post also frames the change as forthcoming — *"From 1 July
2026, Australian employers must…"* — when it has been in force for nearly two months.

Both need correcting. See [blog-and-seo](../08-channels-and-playbooks/blog-and-seo.md).

### Why it is still the best business-finance trigger available

It is dated, universal to every Australian employer, and it converts a quarterly cash outflow
into a per-payday one. That is a working-capital event, not a compliance curiosity — and it is
already biting, which is better than pending.

**Never give payroll, tax or super advice.** Describe the change; refer to their accountant.

## Unchanged, and confirmed

**APRA serviceability buffer: 3%.** APRA confirmed on 23 July 2025 that macroprudential settings
were unchanged, and has made no reduction since despite sustained industry advocacy. The buffer
remains a core lending standard as at August 2026.

**First Home Super Saver Scheme: $15,000 per year, $50,000 total.** Confirmed against the ATO.
The site's figures are correct. Concessional contributions taxed at 15%, with a 30% FHSS tax
offset on release.

## Stamp duty — both markets moved

**Queensland**
- Eligible transactions from **1 May 2025**: full transfer duty concession on **new homes with
  no purchase price cap**
- Established homes: full first home concession under **$700,000**, sliding to **$800,000**, none above
- First Home Owner Grant: **$30,000 until 30 June 2026**, then reverts to **$15,000** — so it is
  **$15,000 now**
- From **1 August 2026**: each purchaser claiming a Queensland home concession must be an
  Australian citizen, permanent resident or specified foreign retiree

**New South Wales**
- Full exemption on homes valued at **$800,000 or less**
- Partial concession from **$800,001 to $1,000,000**

**The site's stamp duty calculator states rates current as at 1 July 2025.** It predates the
Queensland citizenship requirement and the grant reversion. **Flag before promoting it.**
