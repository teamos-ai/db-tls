# Lendi and the comparison portals

> **Tier note:** `inferred`. Lendi's headline — **"Your home for home loans"** — was read
> directly; specific panel and speed claims were not retrievable. Treated here as a
> *category* rather than a single company.

**Type:** Digital brokers and comparison marketplaces — Lendi, Canstar, Finder, RateCity,
plus the banks' own comparison tools.

**This is the most important competitor in the folder**, because The Loans Suite already
positions explicitly against it — and because it is the alternative a cold, self-directed
prospect reaches first.

## The Loans Suite's own counter-positioning, verbatim

The business has already written the argument. Twice:

> Comparing home loans is about more than finding the lowest advertised interest rate. But if
> that's what you're into, **don't waste your life on loan comparison sites.**

> **Saves you time and money by avoiding endless comparison sites. We do the legwork.**

> A comparison tool can help you narrow your options, but **it does not replace personalised
> lending guidance.**

That first line — *"don't waste your life on loan comparison sites"* — is the most confident,
most differentiated sentence on the entire website. It should be used far more than it is.

## What the portals win on

- **Speed.** A number in seconds, no phone call, no name.
- **No perceived commitment.** Nobody rings you back.
- **Anonymity.** You can look without admitting you're looking — which matters more than the
  category admits, given the shame dynamic in
  [buyer-psychology](../03-audience-and-icp/buyer-psychology.md).
- **Trust in "impartiality"**, whether or not it is warranted.

## Their structural weakness — and it is absolute

**Nobody advocates for you.** A portal returns a list. It does not:

- know which lender's credit policy fits a casual income, a trust structure or a recent job change
- restructure a deal when a valuation comes in short
- chase an assessor when a finance clause is expiring
- **work overnight to save your settlement after a bank cancels your unconditional approval**

That last one is not hypothetical. It is Samantha G's testimonial, and it is the single
cleanest illustration of the difference between a list and a broker.
See [testimonials](../05-proof-and-evidence/testimonials.md).

The other structural gap: **a comparison site shows you rates, not policy.** The rate is
public; whether a lender will *say yes to you specifically* is not, and that is the part that
determines whether you buy the house.

## How to compete

**Do not compete on speed to a number** — that fight is lost, and matching it invites promises
we cannot make. Instead:

1. **Be fast to a *conversation*.** Speed to a human is a different axis and one the portals
   cannot occupy.
2. **Reframe what the comparison misses:** rate is public, policy is not.
3. **Use the rescue story.** It is the argument, and it is already written by a customer.
4. **Concede what is true.** The site already does this gracefully — *"A comparison tool can
   help you narrow your options, but it does not replace personalised lending guidance."*
   Conceding the portal's real utility makes the limitation credible.
