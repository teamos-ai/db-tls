# Glossary

Terms the audience uses, and how The Loans Suite explains them. Where the site has its own
explanation, that wording is canonical — **use it rather than inventing a new one.**

## Borrower-facing

**Borrowing power / borrowing capacity** — how much a lender may be willing to lend you.
Constrained by income, expenses, existing debts and the lender's own assessment rules,
including a 3% buffer above the actual rate.

**Comparison rate** — a rate that folds in certain fees to make loans more comparable.
Distinct from the advertised interest rate.

**Equity** — *"the portion of your property you effectively own… generally calculated by
subtracting your remaining loan balance from your estimated property value."*

**Usable equity** — *"the amount of equity a lender may allow you to access, subject to their
lending criteria and your ability to repay the loan."* Not all equity is usable.

**Offset account** — a transaction account linked to the loan; its balance reduces the
interest charged.

**Redraw** — the ability to pull back extra repayments already made.

**Fixed / variable / split** — rate structures. Split is part fixed, part variable.

**P&I / Interest-only** — *"Principal and interest repayments help reduce the loan balance
over time, while interest-only repayments may help with short-term cash flow."*

**LMI (Lender's Mortgage Insurance)** — insurance protecting the *lender*, usually payable by
the borrower where the deposit is below a threshold. **Commonly misunderstood as protecting
the borrower. Always clarify.**

**Pre-approval** — a lender's conditional indication, subject to conditions and expiry.
**Never call it "approval".** See [guardrails](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/guardrails.md).

**Conditional / unconditional approval** — conditional means subject to conditions
(valuation, documents). Unconditional means those are satisfied. Samantha G's testimonial
turns on a bank cancelling an unconditional approval — which is why the distinction matters.

**Mortgage stress** — *"a situation where a household is spending a high portion of its income
on home loan repayments. A common guide is… around 35% or more of household income."*

**Stamp duty / transfer duty** — *"a government charge that may apply when you buy property…
The amount can vary depending on the property location, purchase price, property type and
your eligibility for concessions."*

**Serviceability** — whether you can afford the repayments in the lender's assessment.

**Guarantor** — someone using their own property as additional security.

## Construction

**Progress payments / drawdowns** — *"a construction loan usually releases funds in stages as
each part of the build is completed."*

**Build stages** — slab · frame · lock-up · fixing · completion.

**Fixed-price building contract** — *"Most lenders prefer or require a fixed-price building
contract because it helps confirm the cost of the project."*

## Business and commercial

**Working capital** — funding day-to-day operations rather than an asset.

**Secured / unsecured** — *"Some lenders offer unsecured business loans, while others may
require residential property, commercial property, business assets or other forms of security."*

**Low-doc** — *"alternative verification options, depending on the business, asset, loan amount
and overall application strength."*

**Balloon payment** — a lump sum at the end of an asset finance term, lowering repayments and
raising total cost. **Describe neutrally; never present as a saving.**

**BAS** — Business Activity Statement. Commonly requested by lenders.

**Invoice finance** — borrowing against unpaid invoices.

## SMSF

**SMSF** — Self-Managed Super Fund. **New residential borrowing restricted from 10 August 2026.**

**LRBA (Limited Recourse Borrowing Arrangement)** — the structure through which an SMSF
borrows. **Do not explain in detail** — it is a superannuation-law matter for the fund's
advisers.

## Industry / internal

**Broker market share** — brokers' proportion of new residential lending. **81.0%**, March
2026 quarter (MFAA/Cotality).

**BID (Best Interests Duty)** — the obligation to act in the consumer's best interests.

**Aggregator** — the licensee a credit representative operates under, which sets the panel and
compliance framework. Here: Specialist Finance Group.

**Trail** — ongoing commission paid by the lender for the life of a loan.

**Clawback** — lender recovery of upfront commission if a loan is discharged early.
**Internal only. Never client-facing.**

**Front-book / back-book** — new-customer versus existing-customer pricing.

**MIR** — More Information Required. A lender's request during assessment.

**PEXA** — the electronic settlement platform.
