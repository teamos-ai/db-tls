# Market context

## The category is winning

**81.0%** of all new residential home loans in Australia were written by brokers in the
March 2026 quarter — a record, up from 76.8% a year earlier and 55.3% in March 2018.
Australia is one of only three countries where brokers exceed 80% of mortgage lending.

**Strategic implication:** the business does not need to argue that people should use a
broker. Four in five already do. The argument is *which* broker — which moves the whole
messaging job from category education to differentiation. Much of the site's content still
argues the category case; that content is now fighting a won battle.

## Regulatory backdrop

**Best interests duty**, in force since 2021, has professionalised the channel and is
plausibly part of why share keeps climbing. It also constrains everything we can say — see
[nccp-and-best-interests-duty](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/nccp-and-best-interests-duty.md).

**SMSF residential lending restricted from 10 August 2026.** A live change that reshapes one
of the nine product lines. See [home-suite-smsf](../02-offer-and-lending/home-suite-smsf.md).

**Payday Super from 1 July 2026** — super paid with wages, within seven days, replacing
quarterly. A universal cash-flow squeeze on every Australian employer and the single best
dated trigger for the business-finance audience.

**APRA's 3% serviceability buffer** continues to constrain borrowing capacity, and continues
to surprise borrowers who expect to borrow more than they can.

## Conditions the business's own content identifies

From The Loans Suite's blog, published 2025–2026 — these are the client's own reading of
the market and are safe to build on:

- **The "Covid rate trap"** — *"If you bought your home between 2020 and 2022, there's a very
  good chance your loan was assessed under conditions that no longer exist."* A precise,
  targetable cohort.
- **A post-rate-cut equity environment** — an article on accessing home equity after rate cuts.
- **Business lending demand rising**, driven partly by ATO compliance pressure.
- **Commercial property lending at record levels.**
- **Stamp duty abolished for ACT first home buyers from 1 July 2026.**
- **SMEs seeking a "full-stack" finance adviser** rather than several disconnected ones.

## Local markets

**Moreton Bay / Redcliffe, QLD.** Median house price around $950,000 in early 2026, up
roughly 15.9% year on year *(secondary reporting — verify before publishing)*. Historically
an affordable-entry corridor now pricing out first home buyers, which pushes them toward
deposit schemes, guarantors and deposit bonds — precisely what the Enaybl partnership and the
first-home content address. Rail access to Brisbane and continued infrastructure investment
support the growth story.

**Penrith / western Sydney, NSW.** Sydney's affordable-entry corridor, with strong first home
buyer and investor activity. Jessica covers this market.

**Verify both local figures against CoreLogic/Cotality or realestate.com.au before use.**

## Where the demand is heading

Three currents worth building content and campaigns around:

1. **Refinancing and repricing** — the fixed-rate cliff and the front-book/back-book gap keep
   producing a steady flow of borrowers on rates they would not accept if they checked.
2. **Business cash flow** — Payday Super plus ATO enforcement plus supply-chain volatility.
   Dated, universal, and unaddressed by residential-only competitors.
3. **Equity release** — owners in the two local markets are sitting on growth they have not
   quantified. The equity calculator is the right tool and is under-promoted.
