# Bank — objection turns

Short, reusable turns for copy — email, landing page, ad, chat. For the fuller conversational
versions see [objections](../10-faq-and-objections/objections.md).

> **Tier note:** `inferred`. The objections are reasoned, not recorded. Ten transcribed
> discovery calls would confirm or replace this whole file.

## The rule for every turn

**Agree with what is true in the objection first.** Then add the thing they don't know.
Never argue. A turn that starts by contradicting the reader has already lost them.

---

**"I'll just go to my bank."**
> You should ask them — sometimes they'll move, and that's a good outcome with no effort.
> Worth knowing: most lenders price new customers better than existing ones, so what they
> offer to keep you often isn't their sharpest number. And it's one lender's view out of
> sixty. Ask them, and let us look at the same time.

**"What's the catch? How are you free?"**
> No catch. The lender pays us a commission when a loan settles. If nothing settles, we don't
> get paid — and you're not out of pocket either way.

**"I've already been declined."**
> That's one lender's assessment rules, not a verdict on you. Lenders treat income very
> differently — overtime, commission, casual, self-employed. Some count it, some shade it,
> some won't. It's worth a second look.

**"I don't have enough deposit."**
> Twenty percent is a guideline, not a rule. There are government schemes, and lenders vary in
> what they'll accept. Worth finding out where you actually stand before ruling yourself out.

**"I'll do it myself on a comparison site."**
> Comparison sites are useful for narrowing options. What they can't show you is lender
> policy — whether a lender will actually say yes to your situation. That's the part that
> decides whether you buy the house.

**"Refinancing sounds like a hassle."**
> It is, if you do it yourself. We prepare the paperwork, deal with the lender and chase the
> follow-ups. And if it turns out moving isn't worth it, we'll tell you that too.

**"I'm not ready yet."**
> That's fine — most people we talk to aren't buying tomorrow. Knowing your numbers early
> usually makes the decision easier when you are ready. Nothing has to happen after a
> conversation.

**"My situation is too complicated."**
> Complicated is usually just "doesn't fit one lender's standard box". Self-employed income,
> trusts, multiple properties, variable income — that's most of what we do.

**"How do I know you're not just picking whoever pays you most?"**
> Fair question. Brokers have a legal duty to act in your best interests, and every file we
> lodge is documented against that. Ask us why we recommended a particular lender — we should
> be able to tell you clearly.

**"I've been with my bank for years, they'll look after me."**
> Loyalty tends to be worth less than people expect. Most lenders reserve their sharper
> pricing for new customers. It costs nothing to check whether that's happening to you.

**"I don't want a credit check yet."**
> You don't need one to start. We can work through your position and options first — nothing
> touches your credit file until you decide to apply.

**"I'll wait until rates come down."**
> Nobody can tell you where rates go, us included. What we can do is look at whether your
> current loan is competitive *now*, which is a question that doesn't depend on forecasts.

## Turns that must never be used

- ~~"You'll definitely get approved"~~ — never
- ~~"We can get you a better rate than your bank"~~ — an outcome promise
- ~~"Rates are about to rise, act now"~~ — a forecast and a pressure tactic
- ~~"Your bank is ripping you off"~~ — disparagement; describe the mechanism instead
- ~~"We're the best brokers in Brisbane"~~ — unsubstantiated superlative

## Hardship is not an objection

If someone says they cannot afford their repayments, that is **not** an objection to turn.
Stop selling. Refer to their lender's hardship team and the **National Debt Helpline,
1800 007 007**. See [disqualifiers](../03-audience-and-icp/disqualifiers.md).
