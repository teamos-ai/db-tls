## The method

1. **Agree with what's true.** Every objection contains something real.
2. **Add what they don't know.** One fact, not five.
3. **Reduce the next step.** Make saying yes smaller than saying no.
4. **Never argue, never pressure, never promise.**

## First home buyers

**"We don't have enough deposit."**
> Twenty per cent is a guideline, not a rule — a lot of people rule themselves out on it. There
> are government schemes, and lenders differ in what they'll accept. The useful thing is to
> find out where you actually stand, which takes one conversation and costs nothing.

**"We're not ready yet — maybe next year."**
> That's completely normal, most people we talk to aren't buying tomorrow. Knowing your numbers
> early usually makes it easier when you are ready — you know what you're saving toward instead
> of guessing. Nothing has to happen after a chat.

**"What if we get rejected?"**
> A no from one lender is that lender's assessment rules, not a permanent verdict. That's part
> of why we look across sixty-odd of them rather than one. And nothing touches your credit file
> until you decide to apply.

**"Isn't a broker going to push me into something?"**
> Fair thing to ask. Brokers have a legal duty to act in your best interests, and every
> recommendation is documented against it. If we ever suggest a lender, ask us why — we should
> be able to give you a straight answer.

## Refinancers

**"I'll just ask my bank."**
> You should. Sometimes they move, and that's the easiest possible outcome. Two things worth
> knowing: most lenders price new customers better than existing ones, so what they offer to
> keep you often isn't their best. And it's one lender's view. Ask them and let us look at the
> same time — then you're comparing their offer against the market.

**"It sounds like a lot of hassle."**
> It is, if you do it yourself. That's most of what we do — the paperwork, the lender, the
> follow-ups. And if it turns out moving isn't worth it once we've looked at the costs, we'll
> tell you that.

**"Won't the fees eat any saving?"**
> Sometimes they do. There can be exit costs, application fees and government charges, and
> they're a real part of the maths. That's exactly what a review is for — to find out whether
> it stacks up before you commit to anything.

**"I've been with them fifteen years, they look after me."**
> Loyalty tends to be worth less than people expect in lending. Most lenders keep their sharper
> pricing for new business. It costs nothing to check whether that's happening to you.

## Investors

**"I've hit my borrowing limit."**
> With one lender, maybe. Lenders assess income, rental income and existing debt very
> differently from each other — that's a large part of why panel breadth matters at your stage
> rather than at the first purchase.

**"My accountant handles my structure."**
> Good — they should, and we'd want to work alongside them rather than around them. What we
> handle is the lending side and making sure it fits what they've set up.

**"I'll wait for rates to drop."**
> Nobody can tell you where rates go, us included. What we can look at is whether your current
> lending is competitive now, and whether the structure still supports what you want to do
> next. Neither of those depends on a forecast.

## Business owners

**"My financials aren't up to date."**
> Very common, and rarely fatal. Some lenders offer low-doc or alternative verification
> depending on the business and the amount. Worth knowing what's possible before assuming
> you're stuck.

**"I don't want to put my house up."**
> Understandable. Not all business lending is secured against residential property — some
> lenders offer unsecured facilities, others take business assets or commercial property.
> What's available depends on the lender and the situation.

**"I don't have time for this."**
> That's the point of using us. You give us the information once, and we deal with the lenders.

**"My bank knows my business."**
> They know your accounts. They also only have their own products. Worth having someone who can
> look at what other lenders would do with the same numbers.

## Referral partners

**"I already have a broker."**
> Most people we talk to do. We're not asking you to move anything — happy to just be the
> second name for when your usual one is flat out or a file doesn't fit them.

**"What's in it for me?"**
> Mostly that your client gets looked after and you hear about it before they do. We also refer
> the other way — we publish a partner directory and send clients to it.

**"I don't want to be chasing updates."**
> That's the main thing we'd want to get right. You'd hear from us when your referral moves,
> without asking.

## The objection that is not an objection

**"I can't afford my repayments."**

This is not a sales objection. Stop selling. Their lender has a hardship team, and the
**National Debt Helpline is 1800 007 007** — free financial counselling, which the business's
own site already points people to. Refinancing someone in genuine distress can make their
position worse. See [disqualifiers](../03-audience-and-icp/disqualifiers.md).
